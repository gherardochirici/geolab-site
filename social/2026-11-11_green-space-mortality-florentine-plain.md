## LinkedIn

🌿 More urban green space is a preventive public health strategy: in the Florentine plain (Tuscany, Italy) it could prevent an estimated 346 deaths per year among adults over 35.

The study, published open access in Stochastic Environmental Research and Risk Assessment, covers 452 km², eight municipalities and 3,412 census tracts. It combines Landsat satellite data (NDVI within 300 m of homes), 2021 mortality data and an index of socio-economic deprivation.

📊 Five greening scenarios were compared. An NDVI of at least 0.5 in every census tract would prevent 346 deaths per year (4.9% of the total). An NDVI of at least 0.7 would prevent 877 (12.5%). The probability that more green space has an impact greater than zero exceeds 90%.

⚖️ The more deprived neighbourhoods have less green space. Scenarios based on a minimum threshold of greenness benefit them most, while a proportional increase of 20% mainly benefits neighbourhoods that are already green.

🌳 Indications for planning: combine satellite indicators with the actual types of vegetation, plant trees and create parks first in dense and deprived neighbourhoods, and use green roofs, green walls and pocket parks in historic centres such as Florence.

🏛️ geoLAB, the Laboratory of Forest Geomatics of the University of Florence, is among the authors: M. Baccini, G. Burbui, D. Nuvolone, C. Borghi, A. Lachi, S. Mancuso, G. Chirici.
