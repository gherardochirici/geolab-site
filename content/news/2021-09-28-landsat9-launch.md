<p>Last 27th September, the new Landsat 9 satellite was launched from Vandenberg Space Force Base near Lompoc, California.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p><em>"Continuing the critical observations made by the Landsat satellites is important now and their value will only grow in the future, given the long term environmental changes we are seeing on planet Earth”</em></p><cite><em>John Grunsfeld, former NASA Associate Administrator for Science</em></cite></blockquote>



<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-full"><img src="/media/2021/09/Landsat_timeline_20200318_title.gif" alt="" class="wp-image-1317"/><figcaption>2021 and beyond</figcaption></figure></div>



<p>Landsat 9 carries two science instruments. Both instruments have sensors with moderate spatial resolution (15 m, 30 m, and 100 m depending on the spectral band) and the ability to detect a higher range in intensity than Landsat 8 (14-bit radiometric resolution vs. Landsat 8's 12-bit radiometric resolution). Landsat 9 will be placed in an orbit that is eight days out of phase with Landsat 8, to increase temporal coverage of observations.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-pullquote"><blockquote><p>Landsat 9 in a nutshell:</p><cite><strong>Science Instruments:</strong> OLI-2; TIRS-2<br><strong>OLI-2 build:</strong> Ball Aerospace &amp; Technology Corp.<br><strong>TIRS-2 build:</strong> NASA Goddard Space Flight Center<br><strong>Design Life:</strong> 5 years<br><strong>Spacecraft Provider:</strong>Northrop Grumman<br><strong>Image Data:</strong> > 700 scenes per day<br><strong>Launch Date:</strong> Sept. 27, 2021<br><strong>Launch Vehicle:</strong> United Launch Alliance Atlas V 401<br><strong>Orbit:</strong> near-polar, sun-synchronous at an altitude of 438 miles (705 km)<br><strong>Orbital Inclination:</strong> 98.2˚<br><strong>Spacecraft Speed:</strong> 16,760 mi/hr (26,972 km/hr),<br><strong>Consumables:</strong> 10 years</cite></blockquote></figure>



<p><strong>The new NASA mission will enable informed decision support for key areas such as:</strong></p>



<ul><li>Tropical deforestation and global forest dynamics</li><li>Urban expansion</li><li>Water use</li><li>Coral reef degradation</li><li>Glacier and ice-shelf retreat</li><li>Natural and man-made disasters</li><li>Climate change</li></ul>
