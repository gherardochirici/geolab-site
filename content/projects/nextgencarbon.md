NextGenCarbon is a Horizon Europe project funded under call HORIZON-CL5-2024-D1-01-07 (Grant Agreement 101184989). It started on 1 January 2025 and runs for 60 months, until 31 December 2029. The project is coordinated by the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (Sveriges lantbruksuniversitet, SLU), and the consortium includes 22 institutions from 11 European countries.

NextGenCarbon develops the next generation of terrestrial carbon cycle models by combining Earth Observation data, in situ measurement campaigns, vegetation demographic modelling and data assimilation techniques. The aim is a better-informed assessment of carbon stocks and fluxes in terrestrial ecosystems, to track whether European ecosystems remain carbon sinks under changing climate, land use and management, and to support the update of global and European carbon budgets and EU climate policy.

The work is organised in nine work packages, covering in situ supersites and remote sensing integration, large-scale spatial monitoring of key ecosystems, vegetation demographics, the representation of natural and anthropogenic disturbances in land surface models, data assimilation, the reconciliation of greenhouse gas budgets with national inventories, and scenario simulations of land carbon management strategies.

## The role of geoLAB

The University of Florence takes part in NextGenCarbon with geoLAB, the Laboratory of Forest Geomatics of the Department of Agriculture, Food, Environment and Forestry (DAGRI). geoLAB leads, together with the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), the work package on large-scale spatial analysis for monitoring key terrestrial ecosystems, and leads the task on forest disturbance dynamics: mapping forest disturbances across Europe from Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and Landsat time series, distinguishing rapid events such as windthrows and wildfires from slow processes such as drought and insect outbreaks, and characterising disturbance regimes and post-disturbance recovery from Earth Observation estimates of above-ground biomass and field measurements.
